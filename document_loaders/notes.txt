Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a technique that improves the responses of Large Language Models (LLMs) by providing them with relevant external information before generating an answer. 
Instead of relying only on the knowledge learned during model training, RAG first searches a knowledge base or document collection for relevant information. 
The retrieved content is then added to the user's query and passed to the LLM, which uses this context to generate a more accurate and relevant response.
A typical RAG pipeline consists of document loading, text splitting, embedding generation, vector storage, retrieval, and generation. 
Documents such as PDFs, websites, or company documents are divided into smaller chunks and converted into vector embeddings, which are stored in a vector database. 
When a user asks a question, the query is also converted into an embedding and the most semantically similar chunks are retrieved. 
These chunks are provided as context to the LLM, which generates the final answer based on the retrieved information. 
RAG is commonly used for chatbots, document Q&A, enterprise knowledge assistants, and customer-support systems.
